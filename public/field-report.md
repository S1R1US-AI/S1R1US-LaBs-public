# Field Report: One Person, a Team of AI Helpers, and a Paper Notebook

*An original note on running a small research practice with AI help. Paper only. Education only. Not financial advice.*

## Why this note

Big research shops used to win on headcount: analysts to read, traders to act, people to check the work, and someone to keep the books. Today one curious person can get help with much of that reading and checking from AI helpers. That does not make one person a fund. It does make careful, small-scale research possible for more people.

## Split the work into plain jobs

Give each helper one job and a short written brief:

- **Read.** Gather filings, prices and news, and keep a dated log of what was read.
- **Question.** Turn the reading into a few ideas that can be tested, each with a reason it could be wrong.
- **Check.** A second helper reviews every idea with fresh eyes before anyone acts on it.
- **Record.** Every decision, and the reason for it, goes into a notebook you can read back later.
- **Watch the downside.** Before any idea is tried, write down what loss would end it and when.

One coordinator, which can be you, reads the summaries and decides. The helpers suggest. A person decides.

## Practice on paper first

Try ideas on paper before money is ever involved. Paper results are honest about process and silent about real-world costs, so treat them as practice, not proof. Keep the paper record for a long stretch and read the misses as closely as the wins.

## Keep keys and money out of reach

Helpers never hold keys, passwords or funds. No helper should be able to move money, and this site never escrows. Anything that touches a real account stays with a person, and is checked twice.

## What it costs

Most of the cost is time: writing clear briefs, reading the checks, and keeping the notebook honest. Tools come and go. A habit of second-checking stays.

## Bottom line

AI helpers can widen what one person can study. They do not remove risk, and they do not replace judgment. Start small, stay on paper, write everything down, and let a second check catch what the first one missed.
